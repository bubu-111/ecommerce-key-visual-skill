# Finished-image quality rubric (1–5)

Purpose: **to judge whether one finished image is good enough and whether it needs rework**. It differs from `evaluation.md` — that one measures "is this Skill any good" (accuracy / stability / quality / usability), while this rubric rates "**is this image any good**". Both are needed; neither replaces the other.

Mapping to the scoring brief: dimensions 1 and 2 serve "is the information correct and are all the supplied assets present"; dimensions 3 and 4 serve "does the finished image look good"; dimension 5 is the specification floor.

**Prerequisites for scoring**
- No image means no score.
- Every score needs a reason.
- **State who scored**: self-scoring and third-party scoring are both valid, but must be stated (write "self" honestly when you scored your own work). The aesthetic dimensions especially must not be a bare number.
- Record the score and the reason together in the acceptance record.

---

## Dimension 1 · Information accuracy and completeness (1–5)
- **5**: every required piece of information and every asset is present; numbers, units and conditions contain **no errors or omissions**; nothing unsupported has been added; each language version is content-complete.
- **4**: complete and accurate, with a word or two that could read better.
- **3**: one non-core item missing, or a slightly ambiguous phrasing.
- **2**: a required item missing, or an individual number/unit wrong.
- **1**: core information missing, numbers or conditions clearly wrong, or something unsupported added.
- **Deductions**: price/parameters invented; campaign conditions omitted or wrong; a must-use asset not used; efficacy claims added from nowhere.

## Dimension 2 · Information hierarchy and readability (1–5)
- **5**: the core value is grasped at a glance and the hierarchy is clear rather than flat; at thumbnail size both the core theme and any price required to stand out can be read.
- **4**: the hierarchy is clear, with only secondary information slightly competing.
- **3**: the hierarchy is vague; you have to hunt for the point.
- **2**: the hierarchy is chaotic; the point is nowhere to be seen.
- **1**: information is laid out flat with the focus misplaced, or the core cannot be read at thumbnail size.
- **Deductions**: several messages all made the main focus with no single memory point; the price drowned; decoration competing with the subject; a headline broken across the middle of a word.

## Dimension 3 · Composition and visual expression (1–5)
- **5**: composition, typeface, colour and compositing are coherent, in keeping with the communication goal, with a clear memory point.
- **4**: it works overall, with only individual details to refine.
- **3**: the intent is visible but it looks plain or slightly messy.
- **2**: clearly unbalanced or poorly matched to the goal; needs substantial adjustment.
- **1**: composition unbalanced, or the style conflicts with the goal.
- **Deductions**: conflicting typeface pairing; clashing colours; decoration occluding the subject or cutting its outline; a mismatch with the chosen tone.

## Dimension 4 · Asset handling and compositing quality (1–5)
- **5**: the product and logo keep their appearance, proportions and colours; clean edges, intact structural detail, no occlusion or cutting; consistent light colour temperature.
- **4**: good compositing, with individual negligible blemishes on edges.
- **3**: visible blemishes that do not prevent the subject from being recognised.
- **2**: marked blemishes on edges or structure that hurt the look.
- **1**: subject distorted or recoloured, areas that should be transparent are not, leftover shadows or obvious collage marks.
- **Deductions**: redrawn subject; uncleaned edges; wrong transparency handling; asset source and licence not recorded.

## Dimension 5 · Specification and standard compliance (1–5)
- **5**: width/height, format, byte size and safe area all hit the specification confirmed for this task; no clipped text, no placeholders.
- **4**: all met, with only slightly tight safe-area margins.
- **3**: basically met, one thing to adjust.
- **2**: one item not met (e.g. byte size over the limit) and a re-export is needed.
- **1**: wrong size or format, or clipped text or placeholders.
- **Deductions**: inconsistent with the confirmed specification; export format/byte size over the limit; editable source not kept.

---

## How to use this rubric

| Situation | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Any dimension **< 3** | **Must rework**, back to the relevant stage (information goes back to the facts table/copy, hierarchy to the proposal, visuals and assets to production, specification to export) |
| All dimensions **≥ 4** | May be delivered **for review** — whether it passes is still decided by the user; self-scoring does not replace the user's review |
| One dimension **= 3** | Record the problem and the reason, explain it at delivery; fix it if the user asks |
| The user explicitly asks to **see a draft first** | You may deliver it **marked as a draft** with the known problems written down; marking it a draft does not count as "passing", and does not break the rework rule above |
| **No image** | Do not score; write "not scored" and the reason |

When re-scoring the same version you may show the old score alongside the new one and explain whether the change really took effect. **Never invent a score for aesthetics** — a score with no reason, or with no scorer, counts as not scored.
